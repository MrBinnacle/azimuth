# Base Rates Reference

Use when conducting Base Rate Reality Check (Module 7) in STANDARD or DEEP mode.

Purpose: Ground risk estimates in historical distributions, not imagination. Prefer these priors unless the user provides strong contradicting evidence.

These rates are approximate. Treat them as calibration anchors, not precise predictions.

---

## Software Projects

**Budget overruns**
- ~45–55% of software projects exceed original budget
- Median overrun: 20–45% over estimate
- Projects >$10M: overrun rate climbs above 60%

**Schedule overruns**
- ~60–70% of software projects deliver late
- Average delay: 30–50% longer than original estimate
- Fixed-deadline projects have a higher failure rate than phased delivery

**Scope creep**
- ~50% of software projects experience significant unplanned scope expansion
- Most common cause of cascade delay and budget overrun
- Highest risk in projects with no formal change control

**Feature utilization**
- Industry surveys consistently show ~30–45% of shipped features are rarely or never used
- Implication: most software projects build more than is necessary

**Rewrite projects**
- Legacy system rewrites have an estimated 70–80% failure or cancellation rate before full parity with original
- The "big bang" rewrite is among the highest-risk software strategies

## Startups and New Ventures

**5-year failure rate**: ~50% of startups fail within 5 years (US, SBA data)

**10-year failure rate**: ~65–70%

**Top reported causes (post-mortem surveys)**
1. No market need (~35% of failures)
2. Ran out of cash (~29%)
3. Team problems (~23%)
4. Outcompeted (~19%)
5. Product-market fit failure (~17%)
6. Poor pricing / business model (~18%)
7. Regulatory / legal problems (~8%)

Note: These categories overlap. Most failures are multi-cause, not single-cause.

**Series A to exit success**: Fewer than 1 in 10 Series A companies reach a successful exit (IPO or meaningful acquisition)

**Time-to-PMF underestimation**: Founders typically underestimate time-to-product-market-fit by 12–18 months

## Product Launches

**New product failure rate (consumer goods)**: 70–90% within first 2 years

**Enterprise software new feature adoption**: ~40% of enterprise features see <5% adoption within first 6 months

**B2B sales cycle underestimation**: Average B2B deals take 2–6× longer to close than initial sales projections

**Beta-to-GA conversion**: Conversion from beta users to paid customers averages 15–30% for SaaS products

---

## Hiring and Talent

**New hire failure rate (within 18 months)**: 40–50% of new hires are considered underperforming or departed within 18 months (various HR research surveys)

**Primary causes of new hire failure**
1. Culture/values mismatch (~46%)
2. Unclear performance expectations (~26%)
3. Lack of coaching or onboarding (~23%)
4. Wrong skills for actual job demands (~17%)

**Time-to-productivity for knowledge workers**: 3–6 months for individual contributor; 6–12 months for management roles

**Reference check reliability**: Standard reference checks have poor predictive validity. Structured interviews + work samples outperform unstructured interviews by ~2:1 on predictive accuracy.

## Mergers, Partnerships, and Integrations

**M&A value destruction**: 50–70% of mergers fail to create shareholder value

**Integration overrun**: Post-merger integration timelines are underestimated in ~70% of deals

**Partnership dissolution within 3 years**: ~50% of formal business partnerships dissolve within 3 years, most commonly due to misaligned incentives and unequal effort distribution

---

## IT and Infrastructure Migrations

**Cloud migration overrun rate**: ~38% of cloud migration projects exceed budget; ~45% exceed timeline

**Data migration defect rate**: Data migration projects have a defect discovery rate of 20–40% during or after migration — most commonly from undocumented data dependencies or schema mismatches

**Zero-downtime migration success on first attempt**: <50% in most surveyed enterprise environments

---

## Organizational Change

**Change management failure rate**: 60–70% of large-scale organizational change initiatives fail to achieve intended outcomes (McKinsey, Kotter, others)

**Primary failure modes**
1. Insufficient executive sponsorship
2. Middle management resistance
3. Employee burnout from initiative overload
4. Reversion after initial compliance

**Timeframe underestimation for behavioral change**: Lasting behavioral change typically takes 3–6× longer than initial project plans assume

---

## How to Use These Rates

1. Identify which category best matches the initiative.
2. Use the base rate as the prior probability of failure.
3. Ask: "What evidence do we have that shifts this probability up or down?"
4. If no strong evidence exists to shift it, treat the base rate as credible.
5. Surface the base rate to the user when their confidence appears inconsistent with it.

Do not present these rates as certainties. Present them as: "Historically, initiatives like this fail at X rate. What makes this case different?"
