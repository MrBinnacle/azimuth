- **Productivity (partial evidence):** The Microsoft Japan study is the only cited source. One trial, different culture, different work type, one-month duration. Treat as anecdote, not evidence base.
- **Cost neutrality (strong evidence as stated, but incomplete):** The arithmetic is correct on stated terms. The assumption that operational costs remain constant is unstated and unsupported.

## Likely Failure Paths

- Productivity assumption holds in month 1 (Hawthorne effect), decays in months 2-3 as novelty fades → sprint velocity declines → leadership gets pressure from a missed delivery → informal exception-making begins ("just this sprint, we need all five days") → policy erodes before it institutionalizes → team trust damaged because the rollout wasn't honest about conditionality.

- 9-hour compressed days create caregiving conflicts for a subset of the team → attrition among engineers with caregiving constraints → policy designed as a retention benefit produces retention loss in the affected segment.

- Scheduling compression creates cross-team coordination friction (product, design, QA on 5-day calendars) → engineering becomes a bottleneck at team seams → velocity loss attributed to "the four-day week" regardless of root cause → rollback pressure builds on a causal story that may be wrong.

## Highest-Leverage Fixes

1. **Define a 90-day instrumented pilot with pre-specified exit criteria.** Pick one engineering team. Measure baseline velocity for 30 days before, then 90 days after. Define in advance: "If velocity declines more than X% in months 2-3, we pause and review."

2. **Establish a second-order survey at 60 and 90 days post-launch.** Specifically measure: actual hours worked, caregiving friction, satisfaction with compressed format, and intent to remain.

3. **Define the governance mechanism for deadline conflicts explicitly.** Before Q3, the CEO and VP Engineering need to agree in writing on what happens when a key delivery is at risk.

4. **Survey the 8% indifferent cohort directly.** Aggregate "indifferent" may contain concentrated resistance or constraint. A short structured conversation surfaces whether the indifference is genuine neutrality or suppressed concern.
